## Why this file exists

The old master outlined **Nov 13, 2026 to Jan 1, 2027** by week, and **Jan–Dec 2027 by quarter**. That was a weekly scheduling *detail gap*, not a shortage of proposed content. This document covers **every Friday in calendar year 2027**, including Jan 1 (already part of the 2026 holiday program). Because Jan 1 and Dec 31 both fall on Fridays, there are **53 Friday review windows**. These are opportunities to select a release, not 53 mandatory content launches.

Current 2026-11-06 verified inventory: **150 published curated puzzles, 13 published collections, 15 published categories**, plus existing guides/specialty routes; new PR #22 contains **five technically validated but human-unapproved Christmas/winter draft candidates**, and #26 contains **two technically validated but unapproved evergreen pilots**. **There is NOT currently a year of editorial-approved, publication-ready new pages.** The buildout program must construct, review and freeze that inventory well in advance.

## Operating contract: build a polished site ASAP, release content deliberately

1. **Build clock:** Codex builds the full feasible product, reusable print tools, QA systems and substantive source-only content **as quickly as sound**. No waiting until the scheduled Friday to start development; no automatic pause because a milestone finishes. Use isolated worktrees and review PRs; no automatic main merge or production deploy.
2. **Publish clock:** Initially **zero or ONE genuinely new indexable URL per Friday**, and **zero when no worthy candidate passes**. After observing at least 8 weeks of releases and when individual cohorts have usable 28–56 day observations, up to two in an exceptional well-supported week may be considered by an independent reviewer. This is a conservative internal workflow, **not an SEO ranking formula, Google crawl threshold or guaranteed release frequency**. Substantive upgrades to existing pages and standalone UI/tool updates do not require inventing new URLs.
3. **No forced calendar filling.** Dates are editorial options with fallback to existing-page enhancement or maintenance; unused candidate carries forward or is cancelled. Never create a thin page to keep cadence.
4. **No false equivalence between pack and page.** A 5/10-puzzle PDF feature, printer setting, answer sheet, accessible format and puzzle state variation normally belong on an existing canonical and do not require their own indexable URL. A new collection must have numerous actually published, genuinely distinct useful child puzzles, an independent information purpose and usable filtering/navigation.
5. **Keep season-stable URLs.** Reuse already-published Christmas, Valentine, Easter, Halloween, Thanksgiving, Winter and New Year pages in 2027; don't publish /2027, /2028, /free, /pdf, /answers keyword-variant near-duplicates.
6. **Editorial/source requirements:** unique intended solver/audience; vocabulary distinct from existing 150 and planned siblings; correct clue and educational claims; factual/cultural/rights evidence; stable seed and fingerprints; all words placed; matching online and actual Letter/A4 print/PDF and answers; accessible readable UI; real internal links and accurate dates; canonical/robots/sitemap/draft leaks checked; protected ad/print behavior preserved. Technical validation != human editorial approval.
7. **Approval flags:** PROPOSED → RESEARCHED → DRAFTED → TECHNICALLY VALIDATED → EDITORIAL APPROVED → RELEASE CANDIDATE → OWNER APPROVED → PUBLISHED → MONITORED (or CANCELLED). Staged draft content must stay absent from sitemap, production exports, internal links and public registry. If a preview branch publishes a publicly accessible static route, don't mistake noindex for access control.
8. **Seasonal lead:** aim to prepare and selectively release genuinely seasonal pages **4–8 weeks before their event** when possible; if slot is too late for useful discovery, strengthen existing URLs rather than invent last-minute pages. Build the *next* season's inventory well ahead of time.
9. **Product completion:** see separate finalization contract. The website should reach feature-complete, stable, aesthetically polished operation **independently of the cadence of new content**. Release calendar continues after software is considered complete.

### Portfolio balance and capacity

In this **proposed** calendar there are **35 candidate** windows, **15 existing-page/utility enhancement** windows and **3 explicit hold/maintenance** windows. This is a *planning mix*, not a page-count goal. A candidate may be merged into an existing hub, held for months, cancelled, or replaced with a more valuable original puzzle. Different URLs aren't required to produce weekly value for users: better print output, accurate solutions, navigation, speed, sharing and accessibility are valid release outcomes.

Do not schedule 2027 to reproduce the same generic hard-printable topic every week. Distinguish seasonal, adult-hard, classroom, history/geography, science/nature, and large-print outcomes. The observed hard hub drives ~80.9% of Google clicks in the owner-exported Aug–Nov 2026 baseline; diversification is a *hypothesis to validate*, not reason to publish unoriginal pages.

- **Every Friday:** deploy only independently approved content/feature release. Document exact newly indexable URLs (including **zero**), main SHA, Netlify provider deploy, smoke tests, crawlable links and rollback. No production changes on autopilot.
- **At 7/14/28/56/90 days per cohort:** report Google Web clicks/impressions, total clicks excluding dominant hard collection when data allow, Bing separately, indexability, overlap and output failures. Do not draw ranking conclusions from a single week or assert cause for broad traffic changes.
- **Once per month:** audit ready inventory, cancel/merge weak pages before they become debt, review performance and adjust the next quarter's selection. Publishing doesn't require 12 months of immutable calendar promises.

## What Google actually says

Google's current policies prohibit [scaled content abuse and doorway abuse](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) when pages are generated primarily to manipulate rankings or funnel similar queries, irrespective of how they are created. Its [people-first guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) emphasizes useful, original, accurate and satisfying experiences. Google's [indexing FAQ](https://developers.google.com/search/help/crawling-index-faq) does not promise that sitemap submission or a particular cadence produces indexing. **There is no Google-prescribed one-URL-per-week safety rule.** The throttle here is a conservative editorial/operational choice. High-volume publication of excellent differentiated content is not per se a spam violation; drip-feeding low-value duplicates across 53 Fridays remains poor content.
