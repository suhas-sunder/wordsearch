## Inventory and boundaries

`npm run inventory:content` emits a source-derived JSON manifest, not a committed generated snapshot. At this base it contains 344 registry records: 217 published, 105 draft, 22 redirects; 150 published puzzles, 76 draft puzzles; 13 published and 8 draft collections; 12 published and 2 draft guides; 13 specialty tools marked draft/noindex. The 37 hubs include category and other hub records; 15 categories are published and 2 are drafts. `public/_redirects` contains 35 permanent redirects, including 13 historical aliases outside the registry's 22 redirect records. They are emitted separately, not double-counted as registry routes. Static-host audit confirms 217 sitemap URLs. These are source classifications, not claims about editorial quality or actual traffic.

The 90 catalog IDs/references extracted from the planning document include explicit numbered IDs and expanded numbered ranges plus L-A through L-E. They are marked `proposal-or-existing-reference`: the source mixes proposals, references, reuse and deferred features, so this is intentionally not a false per-item readiness ledger. The planning document remains authoritative for details, action, priority and unnumbered candidate ideas. No proposed path is reserved simply by appearing in the catalog. No hundreds of pages or purported human approvals were generated.

New `StagedPuzzleCandidate` data is distinct from `ContentRecord` and has no registration or publication adapter. Candidate checks compare IDs and paths against all registry records plus the redirect file, and compare normalized intent/title, seed and word sets against candidate peers and existing puzzles; 80% word overlap is flagged for review. Intent checks are lexical and cannot establish semantic uniqueness; humans must review canonical ownership and substance. `npm run audit:candidates -- path/to/candidates.json` accepts a JSON array; it is an initial collision tool, not full schema, factual, placement or publication validation. Existing `audit:content` retains its existing checks and all 150 seeded fingerprint tests remain in the test suite.

Lifecycle transitions record supplied evidence; they do not publish or authenticate the person/evidence. They reject unknown states, skipped stages, undated or empty approval, and recording publication without two full commit SHAs and a deployment reference. Rejected drafts may be retired. No review dates or approvals have been supplied. A real publishing change still requires independent human editorial approval, source/fact checks, seed/placement/answer parity, canonical-intent review, release approval, and a separately reviewed route integration.

The zero-exposure tests apply to NEW source-only candidate data. Historical registry drafts are a different existing architecture: some are statically exported with noindex and remain accessible; their existence was not changed or incorrectly represented as zero-export. Tests cover registry, sitemap, search catalog, published topic lists and discovery picks; static export and search-index generation use those public sources. A later content package must run these checks on its actual candidates and exported output.

Local unrelated untracked growth drafts in the original checkout include Countries Around World, US State Capitals, World Landmarks, US National Parks, American Revolution, Civil Rights Movement, Periodic Table and Genetics. They were not copied, imported, changed or treated as published; review possible overlap before H02/H03/H04.
