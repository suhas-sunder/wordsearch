## Decision key

- **Strong candidate for review**: a clear, useful vocabulary or solving task is
  apparent. Remaining review is concrete and bounded; this is not ready-to-publish.
- **Needs substantive improvement**: scope, terminology, evidence or audience
  framing needs work beyond ordinary final copy review.
- **Better merged into an existing page**: retain the useful bank as a collection,
  packet or output option; separate indexable intent is weak or already served.
- **Duplicate or insufficient value**: a seasonal label or minor activity framing
  does not yet justify this proposed standalone asset.
- **Reject**: do not pursue the proposed use. None of the frozen banks needs
  irreversible deletion; specific publication uses can be rejected separately.

All source files mentioned below are under `content/staged/`. Every main row has
one primary decision. Packet reuse is optional and remains editorially gated.

## Publication-copy defect shared across the inventory

`StagedPuzzleCandidate.editorial` currently uses the same `PuzzleEditorialContent`
shape as public puzzle records. Runtime isolation is already intentional and
valuable; a new CMS is unnecessary. However, importing a candidate wholesale at a
future publication step would carry internal instructions into visitor text.
Examples include X04’s “Keep this unapproved,” H01-P’s “human botanical review must
confirm,” T09-B’s reference to the root workspace, and repetitive print notes
about technical/physical review. Those are appropriate source-review notes but
not final introductions or card summaries.

Require an explicit publication projection with independently approved visitor
description, introduction/context, metadata and print help. Preserve the original
candidate/reviewer notes in a source-only manifest. Approving a word bank must not
implicitly approve its whole editorial object. Likewise, preserve necessary
visitor limitations (for example, mixed cell types or explicitly Christian scope)
without copying generic no-claims sentences into every paragraph. Avoid a broad
ban on “should” or “not”: it would reject legitimate instructions and safety text.

There is no evidence here of copied lyrics, commercial character lists or source
illustrations in these banks. That narrow observation does not certify rights,
originality, authorship or every source-reference claim. Generic dictionary home
pages are pointers for future review, not evidence that an entire specialist bank
has been fact-checked. The source explicitly says this in several families.
